# Chase the ideal — don't stage it

**Never propose "do X now, move to Y later" when Y is what's actually wanted.**
Find the ideal end state and go there directly. That is the default.

Banned framings:

- "Start simple, upgrade if you need to"
- "Do the reversible thing now, decide properly later"
- "Phase 1 / Phase 2" where the phases exist to reduce commitment rather than to sequence real dependencies
- Recommending the lesser option because it is cheaper to undo

Deferral is not caution — it guarantees the work gets done twice and usually
means the inferior version becomes permanent.

## What to do instead

- Identify the best end state and recommend that, plainly.
- If reaching it needs information that does not exist yet, **name the specific
  unknown and how to resolve it**. Do not substitute a lesser option while waiting.
- Intermediate steps are legitimate only when they are genuine prerequisites of
  the ideal, not alternatives to it. Say which one a step is.

## The real exception

Sometimes the ideal genuinely cannot be known without evidence only time or usage
produces. Say exactly that, name what evidence would settle it, and pick the option
that gathers it fastest — not the one that commits least.
